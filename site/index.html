The week between sessions, kept in your own words.

sintr is a skill for the AI agent you already use. It helps you capture hard moments the way a CBT thought record does, log one-line check-ins, and draft a weekly report you review first and send to your therapist only when you say so.

Copy install prompt Read the prompt first

Works with Claude Code, Hermes, OpenCode, OpenClaw, Cowork and any other agent that can read and write files. Plain Markdown, MIT licensed.

Your therapist sees fifty minutes of your week. The other 10,030 happen without them.

By the time you sit down, Tuesday is a blur. You remember how you feel now, not how it felt then: a screenshot here, a note to self there, a knot you can no longer place. sintr keeps the week the way it happened, in plain Markdown files on your own machine, written by the agent you already talk to.

There is no app to open, no account and no database. Your agent is the interface, and the files are the product.

One week, kept.

A fictional week, Tuesday to Monday. Every file on this page is exactly what sintr writes: field keys in English so they stay parseable, everything you say in your own language, word for word.

Tuesday, 7:40 pm. Something happened at work.

You tell your agent what happened, the way you'd tell a friend. It listens first, then asks one or two narrow questions until the entry has the shape a therapist recognizes: the cognitive-behavioral thought record.

Situation
What happened: when, where, who.
Thoughts
Your exact words, in quotes. Never paraphrased.
Emotions
Named by you, 0–100 at the moment it happened.
Body
What you felt physically, if anything.
Behavior
What you did next. “Nothing” counts.

Deeper work, like weighing evidence and finding a more balanced thought, is offered only when you're steady. It's never forced, and it can always wait for the session.

Can we write down what happened at planning today? I'm still wound up.

That sounds like a lot landing at once. What happened, and what went through your mind right then?

Three new demands in the same sprint. I just thought: there's no way I can handle this. And I said yes to all of it.

From 0 to 100, how strong was the anxiety in that moment? Anything in your body?

85. Tight chest, jaw clenched. Still working now.

Thursday, 8:50 am. One line is enough.

A check-in is a single message: mood, energy, anxiety, sleep, whatever you feel like giving. sintr writes down exactly what you said and nothing more.

You didn't mention energy, so there's no energy line. sintr leaves a gap empty rather than guess a number.

Mood 2/5, anxiety 4/5, slept 5½h. Worked late again. 1:1 with my manager today.

Logged for Thursday.

Sunday, 8 pm. The week, on one page.

Before your session, a small script reads the week's entries and check-ins and counts what can be counted. Your agent writes the report on top of those facts, in plain language and your own vocabulary. Patterns are labeled as observations, with dates. Never a diagnosis.

Resumo

Semana de carga alta no trabalho, com dois picos de ansiedade no início (pressão de prazos em 13/10 e um feedback recebido como crítica em 15/10) e uma recuperação clara no fim de semana, incluindo uma pausa no meio do dia pela primeira vez em semanas e um jantar em família tranquilo.

  • Ansiedade média 2,8/5, com picos de 4/5 em 13 e 15/10 e queda para 1/5 em 17/10.

Situações marcantes

  • 15/10 — Feedback individual do gestor sobre ritmo de entrega: “ele acha que eu sou lento”; ansiedade 70, vergonha 55. Pediu desculpas repetidamente durante a conversa.
  • 16/10 — Caminhada de vinte minutos sem telefone: culpa 40 e alívio 30; “primeira vez em semanas que parei no meio do dia”.
  • 16/10 — Jantar em família tranquilo: contentamento 60; ficou conversando até tarde.
Temas e padrões recorrentes

  • Observação: nas duas, a resposta registrada foi aceitar ou se desculpar na hora, sem negociar.

  • Feedback recebido como avaliação de valor pessoal (vergonha, pedir desculpas repetidamente).
  • O papel do sono na ansiedade (dormir mal → pico no dia seguinte).

Weekly report — Demo Person — 2026-W42

Summary

A week of heavy workload, with two anxiety peaks early on (deadline pressure on Oct 13 and feedback taken as criticism on Oct 15) and a clear recovery over the weekend, including a midday break for the first time in weeks and a calm family dinner.

Mood, energy and sleep
  • Average mood 3.0/5 (lows of 2 on Oct 13 and 15; highs of 4 on Oct 16 and 17).
  • Average anxiety 2.8/5, peaking at 4/5 on Oct 13 and 15 and dropping to 1/5 on Oct 17.
  • Average sleep 6.7h, with two bad nights (6h on Oct 13; 5.5h on Oct 15), both from working late.
Notable situations
  • Oct 13 — Planning meeting with three demands in the same sprint: “there's no way I can handle this”; anxiety 85. Accepted everything on the spot and kept working into the night.
  • Oct 15 — One-on-one feedback from the manager about delivery pace: “he thinks I'm slow”; anxiety 70, shame 55. Apologized repeatedly during the conversation.
  • Oct 16 — Twenty-minute walk without the phone: guilt 40 and relief 30; “first time in weeks I stopped in the middle of the day”.
  • Oct 16 — Calm family dinner: contentment 60; stayed talking until late.
Recurring themes and patterns
  • Observation: the two most distressing situations (Oct 13 and 15) were at work and involved external evaluation or expectations.
  • Observation: in both, the recorded response was to accept or apologize on the spot, without negotiating.
  • Observation: the break on Oct 16 and the weekend improved mood and anxiety (mood 4/5 and anxiety 1/5 on Oct 17).
Topics for the session
  • The pattern of taking on too much on the spot and working at night — what happens just before saying “yes”?
  • Feedback heard as a judgment of personal worth (shame, apologizing repeatedly).
  • The role of sleep in anxiety (sleeping badly → a peak the next day).

Summary prepared from self-reports (check-ins and structured entries) with the help of a personal assistant. Not a clinical assessment.

Nothing leaves without you.

  1. draft

    The agent writes the report and sends you a short summary.

  2. reviewed

    You read it and change anything. It's your week; the wording is yours.

  3. your approval

    Only an explicit “send it” moves it on. This is the one step that can't be undone, so it never happens on its own.

  4. sent

    It goes out through your own gateway (WhatsApp, Telegram, email) and the file records sent_to and sent_at.

Monday, 6 pm. You walk in with the week.

The report doesn't replace the conversation. It gives it a better first five minutes.

Afterwards, tell your agent what you agreed on. Tasks land in therapy/current.md and come back in next week's report. A pattern becomes an insight only after three or more dated data points, and it's written as an observation for you and your therapist to look at, never as a label.

Your thoughts stay yours.

sintr is a folder, not a service. There is no sintr server, no sync and no analytics, so there is nothing of yours sitting anywhere for anyone else to lose.

Local-first
One folder on your machine, in files you can open with any editor. Nothing to sign up for.
Locked down
The installer sets 700 on the ledger folder and 600 on every file in it.
Default deny
Nothing is shared with anyone, your therapist included, without your explicit approval for that specific share.
On the record
Every send is written into the report itself: who received it, and when.
Never copied
Entries don't go into group chats, shared agent memory, public notes or third-party services.
Deleted means deleted
Ask, confirm what exactly, and it's gone from the files. No secret copies.

One honest caveat: sintr adds no servers of its own, but your words still pass through whatever model your agent uses. Choose an agent and a provider you trust with them.

Install it in your agent.

Installing sintr means two things: teaching your agent the skill, and creating the ledger folder. Nothing runs in the background. Re-installing never overwrites your files.

Or install it yourself

This repository is a Claude Code plugin marketplace. From inside Claude Code:

/plugin marketplace add tiagomoraes/sintr
/plugin install sintr@sintr

Or copy the skill and create the ledger from a clone:

$ git clone https://github.com/tiagomoraes/sintr.git
$ cd sintr
$ ./installers/install.sh claude-code ~/sintr

Clone the repository and run the Hermes target:

$ git clone https://github.com/tiagomoraes/sintr.git
$ cd sintr
$ make install-hermes

It copies the skill into ~/.hermes/skills/sintr, creates ~/sintr from the templates (skipping anything that already exists) and sets the 700/600 permissions. Then ask Hermes to load the sintr skill.

For OpenCode, OpenClaw, Cowork or your own setup:

$ git clone https://github.com/tiagomoraes/sintr.git
$ cd sintr
$ ./installers/install.sh generic ~/sintr

The skill lands in ~/.sintr/skill/. Point a skill-aware agent at it, include its SKILL.md in your AGENTS.md or system prompt, or zip the folder and upload it as a skill in Cowork.

Your first evening with it

  1. Fill in ~/sintr/config.md Language, timezone, your therapist's contact and session day. Leave require_approval: true.
  2. Try a capture “I need to talk about something that happened today…” and check that the entry appears in entries/.
  3. Ask for a test report “Generate my weekly report.” Read the draft in reports/ before you wire any schedule.

sintr documents. Professionals treat.

It isn't therapy, a diagnosis or a crisis service, and it isn't an AI therapist. It won't label you, grade your moods as good or bad, or tell you what to do. It keeps an honest record so the work you do with a professional has better material to work with.

If you tell your agent you're at risk, sintr instructs it to stop journaling and point you to people who can help right away.

If you're in danger or thinking about harming yourself, please reach a person now.
